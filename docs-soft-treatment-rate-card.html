Drapery priced per cut

Drapery is priced by the cut — the number of 54″ fabric widths the job takes — not by finished size. Get the cut count right and everything else follows.

The calculation, in order

allowance = return × 3 4″ return → 12″ · 6″ return → 18″ cuts = ceil( (rod width + allowance) × fullness ÷ 54″ ) minimum 2 cuts

The allowance goes on before the fullness multiplier — that ordering is what decides whether a job needs one more width of fabric.

  1. Count the cuts

    Formula above. Fullness depends on the pleat style — see the table below.

  2. Apply the base rate per cut

    $125 unlined, $135 lined. Goblet or Barrel pleat adds $20 per cut.

  3. Add the length band

    Based on finished length, added to every cut.

  4. Add interlining, fabric, lining and trim

    Only what applies. Yardage per cut is (length + 16″) ÷ 36.

  5. Apply the $200 set minimum, then shipping

    The minimum is per drapery set. Shipping is a flat $100 or $300 — see Shipping.

Permanently pleated — the one exception

Permanently pleated shades use height × 3 for fabric yardage, because the folds eat the extra. That triple applies to the fabric only — the labour is still charged on the shade's true square footage, not the tripled figure.

The drapery steps

A drape folds into a carton, so a width cut-off alone would be wrong — it is the whole finished panel that sets the box. Both dimensions have to fit a step to earn it.

Finished panelShippingExample
up to 180″ wide × 150″ long$200100″ × 96″ · 36″ × 84″ · 180″ × 150″
up to 250″ wide × 200″ long$300181″ × 150″ · 220″ × 180″ · 250″ × 200″
larger than that$500251″ × 200″ · 300″ × 185″ — freight confirmed at order, may increase

The steps are inclusive — exactly 180 × 150 is still $200, and exactly 250 × 200 is still $300. One dimension over is enough to move it up: 180″ × 151″ is $300. Drapery may be ordered to 300″ wide; length stops at 185″, where the per-cut rate ladder ends, and longer is a hand quote.

Each product steps differently, on purpose. Drapery starts at $200 and steps on the whole panel. A Roman starts at $100 and also steps on the whole shade, because it folds into its carton and the drop matters. A cornice or valance steps on width alone — the board is rigid, so its length sets the box and the face height is irrelevant. So a 90″ Roman ships $200 while a 90″ cornice ships $300.

Splicing saves $200 — or $400

A cornice or valance over 80″ can be spliced: the board is jointed, the fabric stays one piece, and it ships parcel at the $100 base.

An 84″ × 16″ cornice is $760 unspliced against $660 spliced. A 260″ board is $2,110 against $1,710 — a $400 saving. The default on the form is unspliced, so the freight is quoted rather than quietly left out.

Norman ships on its own table — never this one

Norman products (cellular, roller, faux and real wood, verticals) charge $25 first unit + $11 each additional, or $80 + $50 each at 90″ and over. That is Norman's freight. The $100/$300/$500 ladder is ours, for what we fabricate, and a Norman order must never get both.

One size limit still bites

Drapery length stops at 185″ — that is where the per-cut rate ladder genuinely ends, so a longer panel is submittable but carries no figure rather than an invented one. Selling longer needs per-cut rates above 185″.

Everything else is reachable: drapery goes to 300″ wide, Romans to 300″ × 250″, boards to 300″ wide.

One thing to confirm: the Roman minimums above are $150 flat/relaxed and $250 permanently pleated, taken from the site code on both sites. An older note recorded $85 — treated here as the stale figure, but worth a second's thought.

Not included above: Norman-manufactured products price from Norman's own books: Soluna roller, Portrait cellular, faux wood and real wood at retail less 25%; every other Norman product (PerfectSheer, SmartDrape, City Lights, Synchrony, Centerpiece, shutters) at full retail.
